# Georgia Small-Business Registration Research

Use this reference when researching formation or licensing for a Georgia service business. It captures a durable verification pattern, not legal or tax advice.

## Separate the Four Layers

Do not use “business license” as though it were one filing:

1. **Entity formation** — Georgia Secretary of State (LLC/corporation).
2. **Federal tax identity/election** — IRS EIN and any later tax election.
3. **State tax accounts** — Georgia Department of Revenue; only the tax types actually triggered by the business.
4. **Local authority** — city or county occupation tax certificate, zoning, and possible home-occupation approval.

The physical operating location determines the local authority. Determine whether the address is inside incorporated city limits; otherwise check the county. Never finalize local requirements from a city name or mailing address alone.

## Address Roles: Do Not Conflate Them

For a Georgia LLC:

- **Principal-office mailing address:** Georgia Rule 590-7-21-.03 permits a PO box or street address.
- **Registered office/agent:** must be a physical Georgia street address where service of process can occur; no PO box, mail drop, post-office drawer, or rural route.
- **IRS:** a PO box may serve as the mailing address, but the physical business location is supplied separately.
- **Local license:** generally requires the actual operating location for jurisdiction and zoning; a registered-agent service does not replace it.
- **Bank/payment processor:** commonly requires a physical address even when a PO box is used publicly.

A privacy-conscious pattern is PO box for public mailing, commercial registered agent for service of process, and the real operating address disclosed only where required. Do not promise that a virtual office qualifies as the operating location; verify local zoning and licensing rules.

## Service-Business Defaults to Verify

For an owner-operated consulting/marketing/automation business:

- Compare sole proprietorship, single-member LLC, LLC with later S-corporation election, and corporation.
- Explain that an S-corporation is a tax election, not necessarily the state-law entity form.
- Do not recommend an S election solely from revenue. Assess stable net profit, reasonable salary, payroll/accounting costs, and CPA guidance.
- Check whether the actual services fall under a Georgia professional licensing board. Ordinary management, marketing, SEO, CRM, and automation consulting generally differ from regulated legal, accounting, engineering, insurance, or contracting work.
- Georgia generally exempts most services from sales tax, but verify product bundles, hardware, resale software, taxable services, and employees before concluding no DOR account is needed.
- Identify a defensible NAICS code from the actual primary activity rather than choosing “technology” or “AI” from branding alone.

## DBA and Name Checks

- Georgia trade names/DBAs are generally handled by the Clerk of Superior Court in the relevant county, not the Secretary of State, with required newspaper publication.
- Determine whether the public/invoicing name materially differs from the legal entity name.
- A Georgia corporation database search is only preliminary. The state warns that its search is not an official name-availability determination. Report “no exact record found” rather than “the name is available.”

## Source and Verification Order

Prefer current official sources in this order:

1. Georgia statutes/rules and current Secretary of State filing guide.
2. Current city/county application package for the exact physical location.
3. Georgia DOR pages for taxability and account registration.
4. IRS instructions for EIN and tax elections.
5. FinCEN for current BOI obligations.

When official overview pages conflict on a fee, prefer the current detailed SOS filing guide or fee schedule, disclose the discrepancy if material, and avoid presenting an unverified total. Separate formation fees, annual registration, DBA/publication, registered-agent service, and local-license costs.

## Deliverable Shape

Provide:

1. Recommended structure with explicit assumptions about ownership.
2. “Required now / conditional / later” checklist.
3. Exact information needed to file.
4. Address/privacy implications.
5. Local-license dependency and the one missing fact needed to finish it.
6. Annual compliance calendar.
7. Official source links and a brief legal/tax-information disclaimer.

If ownership, home-based status, public brand, or physical jurisdiction changes the recommendation, state the assumption and ask only for those facts.
